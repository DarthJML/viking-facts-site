The Birch-Bark
Hoard

A detector signal in a field by Sysmä led to two birch-bark boxes of silver — Finland's largest Late Iron Age coin hoard, whose furthest coins are Islamic dirhams from the present-day Turkey and Iran region.

II · The story

Read the lesson

September, 2026. A field by the village of Sysmä, in southern Finland. A metal detectorist named Kalle Lappinen gets a signal. Buried between the stones, about half a metre down: a birch-bark box. Then a second box, twelve metres away. And inside — silver.

Thousands of coins, plus hacksilver, a bracelet, a mount and a bead. About four and a half kilograms in all. The largest Late Iron Age coin hoard ever found in Finland, and some of the coins were struck between the 1000s and the 1050s — the Viking Age.

Now look at the furthest coins in the hoard. They're dirhams — Islamic silver, from the region we now call Turkey and Iran. Silver that crossed a continent to end up buried beside a Finnish lake. The Viking world in one box.
